The twist on the box's planes, in three steps.

The 1×1 box is the reference: from level 2 up every arm starts on +1, rests on −1 and ends on −1, the arm on top at a crossing is on +1 and the one underneath on −1, and the next level's arms start on +1. Before: your tips with every fold end on +1, a height that came from Jev's suggestion and is not the box's. Box planes: the same tips with the box's heights (level 2's ends on 0, which only the twist needs). Fixed: the box's heights and every level turned 45° (tip 67.6 px): the tips that leave no ribbon passing through another where yours still did. The four cameras are linked. The first table reads every plane off the ribbons in rungs of half a thickness; the second counts where the built ribbons' surfaces actually cut through each other.

A rung is half a ribbon thickness, 13 px; level L's plane is the middle of its storey, 52 px above the one below. Red: differs from the box by more than half a rung. The fold end's height gap is measured from the end of a level's arm to the nearest other-lace arm of the same level: 26 px is one full thickness, the ribbons resting on each other; less means the fold end sits inside that arm's thickness. Plan is how far the fold end is from that arm's edge (negative: inside it). Level 10 is the top: its ends are loose.

Ribbons cutting through each other, on the built meshes

ColumnPlaces deeper than 6 pxDeeper than 8 pxDeepest

Every triangle of every ribbon is tested against the others'. Where two cut through each other, the depth is how far the shallower side pokes through the other's plane, in px (a ribbon is 26 px thick). Triangles of one lace near each other along it are not compared, and neither are two laces' two arms resting on each other without crossing through. The box has some of these where ribbons rest on each other at a crossing, so it is the reference, not zero. It takes about half a minute.

What was changed

BeforeYour tips (61.5, 60.5, 60, 60.5, 60.5, 60.5, 60.5, 65, 62 px). Fold ends of levels 2–9 rest on +1; the box ends them on −1.
Box planesSame tips. Fold ends rest on −1, as in the box; the next level's arms start on +1; crossings ±1. Level 2's ends rest on 0: turned, they land over the corner where a level-1 arm of the other lace leaves its slant; on −1 they graze it (6 px on the centreline test), on 0 they clear it.
FixedBox planes, and every tip 67.6 px: every level turned exactly 45°, the turn measured on the 1×1. Swept from 44 to 78 px with the box's planes, the places deeper than 8 px fall from 164 at 44 px, to 56 at 60 and to 1 at 66–69, and stay there; at 67.6 there are none and the deepest is shallower than the box's. A level isolated from above is then as straight and uncreased as the box's.
Since thenStretching each level's tip to about 75 px (a turn of about 41°, set on twist-1x1-ends) tightened the column further, and level 2's ends went back to −1 like every other level. That is the column in the studio's samples now: over all ten levels 4 places deeper than 8 px against the box's 5.
UnchangedLevel 1 (the box from box + strand). Its four fold ends still come within 5–6 px of a level-2 arm on the centreline test; that is the box's own level 1.
