Who rides over, when nobody says?

On a single plane, with no masks, the studio does one thing at every crossing: the higher layer rides over. Draw a row, then a column, and the whole column sits on top of the whole row. That is a stack, not a weave. Left is exactly what the app builds today. Right is the same strands under a different default. Orbit either one; the other follows.

Proposed

Plain weave
Same masks as the app would write

Each row is one lace read from its start. A filled chip is a crossing it rides over, an outlined one is a crossing it ducks under. These come from the engine's own crossing list, so they describe the canvas, not a drawing of it.

Reading plain weave

Every lace alternates over, under, over, under along its length, and every neighbour lands on the opposite phase. A lace cannot slide out sideways because the laces either side of it hold it down at alternate points.

Reading twill 2/1

Each vertical goes over two and under one, and the pattern steps over by one lace per row, which gives the diagonal rib. It drapes better than plain weave and shows more of one colour, so it makes a poor default but a good pick.

Reading basket 2×2

Pairs of laces move together, over two, under two. It looks chunkier and has fewer tie-down points than plain weave, so the weave is looser. It suits wide laces.

What the studio does today

One rule, decided once per pair of strands

At each pair of strands that cross in plan, a mask picks the one that rides over. With no mask the higher layer in the panel wins. That answer is the same at every place the two cross, and it never looks at what else the strand crosses (StrandScene.weaveCenterlines, over = masked ?? j). The over strand is raised to the storey plane plus half a separation and the under strand is lowered by the same, easing back over a short span either side.

Away from a crossing a lace rests at its layer rank times the layer lift, so a plane of six laces fans out over 50 px even where nothing crosses. Laces on different storeys are not woven at all unless a mask says so. Because the default is per pair, in a 3×3 mat each of the six laces is either over at all three of its crossings or under at all three. Every shipped weave sample works around this by hand: the woven mat, the curved weave, the twist stitch and the box stitch all write a checkerboard of masks.

What would make more sense

1 · Plain weave (recommended)

Alternate along each strand. Number a strand's crossings in order along it. At a crossing, the higher layer rides over when the two numbers add to an even number, under when odd. Two strands that cross once behave exactly as they do now, so the two-crossing demo does not change. Masks still win, and a masked crossing still counts, so the weave stays in step around it.

2 · Basket 2×2

Alternate in pairs. Same counting, but halve each number first. Looser and chunkier. Worth offering as a switch in the Weave panel, not as the default.

3 · Twill 2/1

Over two, under one. Add the numbers and take them modulo three. A texture choice, like basket. It leaves a plane lopsided, with one face mostly one colour.

4 · Keep layer order

Today's rule, kept as an option. Right for a plane that is meant to be a stack, and for any scene saved before this change.

Why plain weave

  • It is what you meant. Everything the app ships on one plane is plain weave that somebody masked in by hand.
  • It is the only option where every lace is held. Under layer order, the verticals can be lifted off as one sheet.
  • It costs nothing visually for two strands, and the engine already anchors heights per crossing, so the geometry needs no change.

What it would touch

  • weaveCenterlines: the over/under choice moves from per pair to per crossing, and needs each strand's crossings counted first.
  • Only same-storey strands count, so level breaks keep meaning what they mean now.
  • CrossingFact is per pair. A pair that flips along its length needs a "mixed" answer, or the fold lab's list would disagree with the picture.

The catch

  • An existing save with no masks would change shape on load. Store the choice on the scene, and read old saves as layer order.
  • A pair that crosses twice, such as a curve looping over a strand, would go over once and under once. That links the two, which is physical, but it is a change from today.
  • A new Weave panel switch would be the place to pick plain, basket, twill or layer order.
