What you are looking at

One 2×1 ring worked left-handed, k = −1 four times over: a block of three laces and then four rounds of six arms, 33 strands and 20 crossings in all. Every round of it was placed by a person in the MXN lab, not searched — and each one closes 8 crossings out of 8.

Two things called a level

The lab's level is an observation of k — the fourth round of this ring is its level 4, and all four are drawn in one flat plane. Scoubidou3D's level is a break in the layer stack: everything above it rests one storey higher, and a storey is two strand thicknesses, because the thing it has to hold is a woven round — one lace riding over, one ducking under.

Where the breaks go

The block is the ground. Each round of six arms is one storey up, so the breaks land at 9, 15, 21 and 27 of the 33 rows — exactly where swirlStitch splits its own continuation off its block. The build refuses to run if a round is not one unbroken run of the layer stack, or if any mask reaches across a break.

What the flat one costs

The same ring imported with no breaks stands — storeys tall against — with them. Every round lands in the plane of the block, so the four of them interlock as one thick round instead of standing on each other, and only the masks keep them from passing through.

Making the cord one cord

An OpenStrand file names the strand each arm hangs off, and the import now reads it — without that declaration a lace carried up onto a new storey is refused at the break, and these 33 strands come apart into 27 unrelated laces. That closes 24 of the 30 joints. The last six are the block's: this ring was fitted before extension 0 moved from an arm's tip to its weave point, so its first round starts 25 to 47 px past the tip it leaves. All six sit exactly on that arm's own line, so the page runs each arm out to meet its round — nothing bends, nothing moves sideways, and the round above keeps the position the fit solved for. — joints close, — arms were run out, and the ring stands as — cords.

Changing it

Move points is the app's own move tool: grab a handle and drag, and every endpoint glued to that one comes with it, so a weld stays welded. The joint row is the same move with its direction fixed — it slides the chosen arm's weld along the arm it leaves, which is the extension the lab dials, and the readout says where it now sits. Step to a storey first and the picker offers that storey's six arms. Copy scene JSON takes the result back to the app or the lab; Reset ring puts it back.
