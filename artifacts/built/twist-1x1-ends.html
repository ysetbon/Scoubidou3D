Set each fold's planes, level by level.

A fold joins the end of an arm on level L−1 to the start of the arm that carries on from it on level L. Pick a level and every one of its four folds has two heights you can set arm by arm: the plane the previous level's arm ends on, and the plane this level's arm starts on, in rungs of half a ribbon thickness. The column is levels 1 to L on the 45° twist, rebuilt as you change them, with that fold's gap to the arm it crosses and a count of where the ribbons cut through each other. Changes stay in this browser. Copy hands every setting, note and coordinate back.

Stretch, level by level

How far past the weave a level's folds sit, in px: the distance from the crossing arm's centreline to the fold, which is where the next level's strands start. 0 would put a fold right on the weave. About 23 px is just clear of the crossing arm's far edge (it is 23 px wide each side of its centre). More stretch turns the next level less and makes the column longer and looser to the eye (80 px of tip is 52 here, and 38.6° a level); less stretch turns it more and draws it in. One number per level, so the four arms of a level stretch together. Outlined: the level whose fold you are setting.
