What you are looking at

One 2×1 ring worked left-handed, k = −1 four times over: a block of three laces and then four rounds of six arms, 33 strands and 20 crossings in all. Every round of it was placed by a person in the MXN lab, not searched — and each one closes 8 crossings out of 8.

Two things called a level

The lab's level is an observation of k — the fourth round of this ring is its level 4, and all four are drawn in one flat plane. Scoubidou3D's level is a break in the layer stack: everything above it rests one storey higher, and a storey is two strand thicknesses, because the thing it has to hold is a woven round — one lace riding over, one ducking under.

Where the breaks go

The block is the ground. Each round of six arms is one storey up, so the breaks land at 9, 15, 21 and 27 of the 33 rows — exactly where swirlStitch splits its own continuation off its block. The build refuses to run if a round is not one unbroken run of the layer stack, or if any mask reaches across a break.

What the flat one costs

The same ring imported with no breaks stands — storeys tall against — with them. Every round lands in the plane of the block, so the four of them interlock as one thick round instead of standing on each other, and only the masks keep them from passing through.

Six joints this save never closed

An OpenStrand file names the strand each arm hangs off, and the import now reads it — without that declaration a lace carried up onto a new storey is refused at the break, and these 33 strands come apart into 27 unrelated laces. With it, 24 of the 30 joints weld. The other six are the block's: this ring's first round starts 25 to 47 px past the tip it should leave from, so the three cords still read as nine pieces. That is the save, not the model — re-dial those pairs in the lab and they close.
