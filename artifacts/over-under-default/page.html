What the studio does today

The top layer rides over, and each lace swings 26 px

With no mask the higher layer in the panel wins (StrandScene.weaveCenterlines, over = masked ?? j). The over lace is lifted Depth above the plane the two share and the under lace is lowered by the same, easing back over 1.3 crossing widths either side. Laces rest at their layer rank times the layer lift when nothing crosses them.

Those defaults mean the two ribbons end up 52 px apart, centre to centre. A ribbon is 26 px thick, so there is a full thickness of daylight between them and the top lace arches over nothing. Nothing in the app says this is the right height. It is the Depth slider's starting value, and Depth only ever floors at the least that keeps them from passing through each other (about 15 px, from the ribbon thickness times 1.15).

Other defaults, and which makes sense

Meet in the middle (recommended)

Orange a bit down, yellow a bit up, touching exactly. Each lace moves half a thickness from the plane, so the surfaces meet with no air and no overlap. It is the same symmetric swing the app already uses, just sized to the one value where the two ribbons rest on each other. Levels already force this: a crossing in a stacked scene is capped at half a thickness either side of its storey, so this default is what a stack gets anyway.

Meet, orange on top

The same contact, other way round. Useful if the lace drawn first should be the one that rides over. It changes who wins at every crossing, so it only makes sense as a per-scene choice.

Under lace stays flat

One lace bends, the other lies on the table. The bottom lace keeps its own height and the top lace arches over it by one thickness. Good when the bottom layer is meant to be a base. The whole weave sits higher, so a storey above it has less room.

Top lace stays flat

The mirror. The top lace stays on the plane and the lower one ducks a full thickness under it. It keeps the top surface level, so it reads best from above. It digs below the plane, so it needs room under the storey.

Resting on it

The smallest change. Keep today's symmetric swing and drop Depth to its floor. That leaves a 15% margin, about 4 px of air, so the laces never quite touch. Meet in the middle is the same idea made exact.

Keep today's, or first drawn on top

Only if the gap is on purpose. A thickness of daylight is easy to read from the top and leaves room for a third lace between them. Flipping who is over changes every new scene.

Why meet in the middle

  • The two laces touch, so the crossing is physically possible, which today's 26 px of air is not.
  • Both laces bend the same amount, so neither one is special and the plane stays the centre of the weave.
  • It matches the half-thickness rule the level system already applies, so a scene with levels and one without behave the same.

What it would touch

  • weaveAmplitude: the default swing becomes half a thickness instead of the Depth value, with no 15% margin.
  • The Depth slider would then add swing on top of contact rather than set it.
  • Same-storey pairs only, so level breaks mean what they mean now.

The catch

  • Scenes people have already built would sit lower. Gate it on a scene setting and read old saves as today's.
  • With no margin, curved or thick laces can show a hairline of z-fighting where surfaces meet. A 1 to 2 px margin would hide that.
