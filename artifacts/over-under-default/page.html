    What the studio does today
    
No plane placed. The top layer rides over and each lace swings 26 px

    
With nothing placed, the higher layer in the panel wins
    (StrandScene.weaveCenterlines, over = masked ?? j). The over lace is
    lifted Depth above the plane the two share, and the under lace is lowered by the same,
    easing back over 1.3 crossing widths either side. When nothing crosses them, laces rest
    at their layer rank times the layer lift.

The two ribbons end up 52 px apart, centre to centre. A ribbon is 26 px thick, so there is a
    full thickness of daylight between them. The weave is also not centred on the storey: yellow
    rests 10 px above orange from the layer lift, so the pair swings about a point 5 px above the
    middle, and the crossing reads +31 and −21 rather than ±26. In planes, today's gap is the
    storey's own floor and ceiling (rungs −2 and +2) and a small offset. Depth only floors at about
    15 px, the thickness times 1.15, to keep ribbons from passing through each other.

    
Two things the planes showed. A run plane alone cannot make the laces touch. Orange on
    −1 and yellow on +1 are exactly one thickness apart, which is less than the 1.15 clearance the
    engine asks for, so the weave swing still gets added and the crossing comes out 52 px apart,
    the same as today. Only a pair three rungs apart (1.5 thickness) counts as already clear and
    skips the swing. So an exact touch needs the crossing placed on the same rungs as well, which
    is the checkbox above.

  


  
Proposals, with the planes each one uses

  

    

      
Meet in the middle (recommended)

      
Orange on −1, yellow on +1, crossing placed on both. Orange sits half a thickness
      below the middle, yellow half a thickness above it, touching exactly: 26 px apart, 0 px of
      air. It is the symmetric swing the app already uses, sized to the one value where the two
      rest on each other. Levels already force this much: in a stacked scene a crossing is capped
      at half a thickness either side of its storey.

    

    

      
Both low

      
Orange on −2 (floor), yellow on 0 (middle), crossing placed. Touching exactly, with
      the whole crossing in the lower half of the storey. The upper half stays free for a lace
      resting on it. Orange sits at −26 and yellow at 0.

    

    

      
Both high

      
Orange on 0, yellow on +2 (ceiling), crossing placed. The mirror: touching, with the
      lower half free underneath. Orange at 0 and yellow at +26, so the weave sits at the top of the
      storey and meets the floor of the one above.

    

    

      
Floor and ceiling

      
Orange on −2, yellow on +2, no crossing placed. Today's 52 px and 26 px of air, but
      declared, so it holds whatever Depth is set to. It is the way to keep today's look on purpose.
      The two are far enough apart that the engine adds no swing.

    

    

      
Half a thickness clear

      
Orange on −2, yellow on +1, no crossing placed. Three rungs apart, so 39 px
      centre to centre and 13 px of daylight with no swing added. The least a run plane can do on
      its own.

    

    

      
Meet, orange on top

      
Orange on +1, yellow on −1, crossing placed, orange rides over. The same contact
      with the roles swapped. It changes who wins at every crossing, so it only makes sense as a
      per-scene choice. "First drawn on top" changes only that, with no planes.

    

  


  

    

      
Why meet in the middle

      
    
        
  • The two laces touch, so the crossing is physically possible, which today's 26 px of air is not.
    • 
        
  • Both laces move by the same amount, and the pair is centred on the storey middle instead of 5 px above it.
    • 
        
  • It scales to a whole weave. Run planes only have seven rungs, but "over lace on +1, under lace on −1 at this crossing" works for any number of laces.
    • 
      

    

    

      
What it would touch

      
    
        
  • weaveAmplitude: the default swing becomes half a thickness instead of Depth, with no 1.15 margin, and the weave plane is the storey middle instead of the midpoint of the two rests.
    • 
        
  • Placing laces on rungs by hand stays as it is, through the Planes view. This only changes what an unplaced crossing does.
    • 
        
  • Same-storey pairs only, so level breaks mean what they mean now.
    • 
      

    

    

      
The catch

      
    
        
  • Scenes people have already built would sit lower. Gate it on a scene setting and read old saves as today's.
    • 
        
  • With no margin, curved or thick laces can flicker where the surfaces meet. A 1 to 2 px margin would hide that.
    • 
        
  • A placed run plane still gets today's swing on top unless it is three rungs from its partner. That is existing behaviour and worth knowing when placing by hand.
