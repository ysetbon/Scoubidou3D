Both views hold the same two strands: h (orange, drawn first, bottom
  layer) and v (yellow, drawn second, top layer), 54 px wide, crossing at 90° in the
  middle, with no level break so they share one storey. The ladder is the studio's own plane
  ladder: a rung is half a strand thickness (13 px here), 0 is the middle of the storey and ±2

  crossing (planes). A solid marker is the rung it takes at the crossing
  (crossPlanes). Heights are read back off the built ribbons, in source pixels,
  against the middle of the storey, and "moves" is how far the lace is from that middle at the

Away from the crossing, the laces do not rest on the middle. Each rests at its layer rank
    times the layer lift, so yellow sits 10 px above orange even where nothing touches it. The
    left view reads "Away from it: h 0 · v +10". That also moves the whole weave: the pair swings
    about a point 5 px above the middle, so the crossing reads +31 and −21 rather than ±26. At the
    crossing the two end up 52 px apart, centre to centre. A ribbon is 26 px thick, so there is a
    full thickness of daylight between them.

  


  
Proposals: rest on the middle, move only at the crossing

  
Every proposal below puts both laces on rung 0 (the middle) away from the crossing, so
  the far parts of the strands are flat and level with each other. Only the crossing is placed
  on other rungs, and each lace eases back to the middle on either side of it. In the right view
  the "Away from it" box reads h 0 · v 0 for all of them.

  

    

      
Meet in the middle (recommended)

      
Rest 0 · crossing: orange −1, yellow +1. Orange dips half a thickness to the lower half
      and yellow rises half a thickness to the upper half, touching exactly: 26 px apart, 0 px of
      air. It is the symmetric swing the app already uses, sized to the one value where the two
      rest on each other, and it leaves both laces centred on the storey. Levels already force this
      much: in a stacked scene a crossing is capped at half a thickness either side of its storey.

    

    

      
Both low

      
Rest 0 · crossing: orange −2 (floor), yellow 0. Touching exactly. Yellow does not move at all
      and only orange dips, a full thickness, so the top surface stays level with the rest of the
      strand.

    

    

      
Both high

      
Rest 0 · crossing: orange 0, yellow +2 (ceiling). The mirror. Orange stays level and only
      yellow rises, a full thickness, so the bottom stays flat on the plane.

    

    

      
Floor and ceiling

      
Rest 0 · crossing: orange −2, yellow +2. A full thickness of air, 52 px apart. It is today's
      gap, but declared and centred on the middle, so it holds whatever Depth is set to.

    

    

      
Half a thickness clear

      
Rest 0 · crossing: orange −2, yellow +1. Three rungs apart: 39 px centre to centre and
      13 px of daylight. Use it where a little room matters more than contact.

    

    

      
Meet, orange on top

      
Rest 0 · crossing: orange +1, yellow −1, orange rides over. The same contact with the roles
      swapped. It changes who wins at every crossing, so it only makes sense as a per-scene choice.
      "First drawn on top" changes only that and places nothing.

    

  


  

    

      
Why this shape

      
    
        
  • Away from a crossing nothing is happening, so the strand lies on the middle of its storey like every other strand. Today a lace's far parts depend on how many layers are below it.
    • 
        
  • The two laces touch, so the crossing is physically possible, which today's 26 px of air is not.
    • 
        
  • It scales to a whole weave. Run planes only have seven rungs, but "rest on 0, over lace +1, under lace −1 at this crossing" works for any number of laces.
    • 
      

    

    

      
What it would touch

      
    
        
  • weaveCenterlines: with nothing placed, a lace rests on the storey middle instead of its layer rank, and an unplaced crossing anchors at ∓½ thickness instead of ±Depth, with no 1.15 margin.
    • 
        
  • weaveAmplitude and the Depth slider would then add swing on top of contact rather than set it.
    • 
        
  • Placing laces on rungs by hand through the Planes view stays as it is.
    • 
      

    

    

      
The catch

      
    
        
  • Resting every lace on the middle drops the layer lift, so a stack of laces that do not cross would overlap. The layer lift would have to stay for strands that never cross anything, or a placed rest plane could apply only near crossings.
    • 
        
  • Scenes people have already built would change. Gate it on a scene setting and read old saves as today's.
    • 
        
  • With no margin, curved or thick laces can flicker where the surfaces meet. A 1 to 2 px margin would hide that.
