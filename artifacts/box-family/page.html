What you are looking at

A box stitch on an m × n face: m ribbons one way crossing n the other, woven, and then every loose end carried straight back across the block and out the far side. The second pass rests a storey on the first — that is the step you can see in the ends, and why a box is thick rather than merely wide.

Why there is only one per size

A box is the twist family's own starting stitch at k = 0. The pointer does not move, so every end pairs with the end straight opposite and each arm carries on along its own line. Nothing rotates, nothing is stretched, and the aligner that a twist needs reports there is nothing to do. No angle to derive, no dial to set — one box per size per hand.

What m ≠ n costs

Not slack: no arm was turned off its line, so none hangs past the weave the way a lopsided twist's does. What it costs is binding. Take the 1×8 — its single warp passes through all 64 crossings while each of its eight wefts passes through 8, so the whole face hangs off one piece of plastic. Square faces share it evenly.

Where this comes from

The meshes were built by the studio itself and lifted out of it, so the page cannot disagree with the app about the model — an 8×8 alone is 4 MB of it, which is why six faces are baked here rather than all 64. Every one of the 64 is a sample in both hands — 8×8 in the studio, ?sample=box-lh-3x2, and the rest — and they are drawn flat, arm by named arm, in the companion sheet Box Stitches.
