What you are looking at

Ten storeys of a 1×1 twist, truncated at level 9 — the same model the contact sheet renders. Two laces, orange and indigo, each folding once per storey.

What went wrong

Nine levels of a 50.03° turn is 450.30° — 90.30° from where it started. By level 8 the arms land within 0.32 px of the base bars in plan while sitting eight storeys above them, and the base bar of one lace was being adopted as a child of the other lace's top arm.

Why the mesh count tells you

A lace that reads as one physical cord is swept as one ribbon. Splicing two colours into a chain breaks that: each lace comes apart into four pieces, and short bridges are lofted across the seams — one of them 1.8 units tall where a storey is 1.0. Four meshes is two laces, each with its dark rim. Sixteen is the damage.

The fix

collectJunctions already refused to let a coincidence override a declared parent. A lace's free root has nothing to declare, so the storey answers instead — the undeclared search is now confined to the level the strand sits on. Three faces of 64 were affected; no other sample moved.
