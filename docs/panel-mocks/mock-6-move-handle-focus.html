Panel mock 6 · Move mode

Handle focus

Move draws a grab mark on every visible layer in the scene, always. On a ten-storey box stitch that is 126 marks over a model twelve laces wide; on the 7×3 twist it is 690. This proposal gives Move one control — a scope — that limits the marks to a single storey or a single layer, without hiding anything.

Box stitch · 10 levels 126 grab marks in Move today — 84 endpoints, 42 control triangles, over 42 layers
Focused on level 6 12 4 layers of that storey: 8 endpoints, 4 triangles. Everything else stays drawn
Twist stitch · 7×3 690 230 layers over 11 storeys — the scene the proposal is really for
Focused on one layer 3 two endpoints and the triangle they share, anywhere in the stack

01 The problem is that every layer answers at once

buildHandles() walks every visible, non-mask strand and gives each one two endpoint spheres, its control marks and a dashed rig. That is right on the opening sample, which has six layers. It stops being right the moment a scene has storeys: the marks of ten rounds land on top of each other in screen space, the pick has to cycle through the pile to reach the one you meant, and the model you are editing is under a fog of blue and green.

02 The control: one scope, three reaches

All · Level · Layer. It says what counts as “in reach of the Move tool”, the way the inspector's Applies to switch says what counts as “this”. It carries a target chip naming the storey or the layer it is pointed at, and a live count of the marks it leaves on screen, so the strip can be read before it is used.

03 Where it goes, and the two places it must not

Three homes were drawn. The question is not which is prettiest but which one is reachable at the moment you need it — which is mid-edit, with a card closed, on a panel that may be showing masks instead of layers.

04 The four states

Every state the strip can be in, drawn against the same scene: the ten-storey box stitch, 42 layers, its level 6 carrying 1_14, 2_14, 1_15 and 2_15.

Handles

All — what ships today

The default, and the state a new scene opens in. No target chip, no count: nothing is being withheld, so there is nothing to report. Six-layer scenes never see this feature at all.

Handles ◎ Level 6 ▾

Level — one woven round

12 marks instead of 126. A level is exactly the unit you edit as a unit: one round of the weave, two thicknesses tall, which is what docs/layer-levels.md says a storey is.

The nine storeys around it are still drawn, still coloured, still in the way of the camera — you are working inside the model, not on a stripped copy of it.

Handles ◎ 1_15 ▾

Layer — one arm

Three marks in the whole scene: two endpoints and the triangle parked on the start. This is the state for bending one arm of a stitch that has forty of them within a few pixels of each other.

Opening a strand's row in the panel sets this target, so the reach you already use to colour a layer is the reach that hands you its handles.

Handles ◎ 1_15 — hidden

The target went away

Hide the focused layer, delete it, or drag the level break out from under it, and the scope has nothing to point at. It says so and falls back to All rather than leaving a canvas with no handles on it and no explanation.

The one failure this feature can have is “I clicked the endpoint and nothing happened”. Every state above is built so that sentence is never true.

05 The stack is the picker

The ◎ is the same mark on a level bar and on a layer row, and it means the same thing in both places: point Move here. It is drawn only while Move is armed, so the panel does not grow a control that does nothing in Orbit.

Why a banner as well

The panel already carries one sticky coral strip — hidden-banner, the way back from a solo — for exactly this reason: a state you can leave running is a state you have to be able to undo without remembering how you got into it. Focus is that kind of state.

It reads Handles on level 6 only with one press back to All, and it appears in the stack even when the strip over the canvas is scrolled behind a card, on a phone, or on a panel switched to Masks.

The press that already exists

Opening a layer's row is already how you reach its colour, its width and its Straighten. With the scope on Layer, that same press points Move at it — so in the common case you never touch the ◎ at all: you open the row you were going to open anyway, and the handles come to you.

06 The rules that keep it safe

A scope that limits what you can grab is one bad rule away from a scene that feels broken. These are the rules that stop that.

01

Focus is not hide

Nothing is dimmed, ghosted or removed from the canvas. Every lace keeps its colour, its weave and its place in the stack. Only the grab marks go — which is the difference between this and Hide others, and the reason both are worth having.

02

A drag still reaches everything glued to it

Move's whole point is that connected endpoints travel together. Focus limits what you can grab, never what can move: drag 1_15's end and the attached 1_16 follows out of the focused storey exactly as it does today. connectedEndpoints is untouched.

03

Off by default, sticky once used

Scope opens on All, so nothing changes for the six-layer samples the project opens with. Once set, it is remembered between visits — like the theme and like the inspector's reaches — because it is how you are working, not a property of the scene.

04

Two ways out, always visible

The strip's All button while the canvas has focus; the stack's coral banner while the panel does. Esc clears to All from either. The scope never survives a state where neither of those is on screen.

05

Attach inherits it; Weave ignores it

Attach draws the same endpoint marks and gets the same scope for free — pulling a new strand off one storey of a twist has the same problem. Weave picks strand bodies, not handles, so it is untouched and shows no strip.

06

The keys walk the stack

[ and ] step the focused storey down and up — which on a twist is how you actually work, one round at a time — and the camera does not move, so the model stays where your eye left it.
