Type the k sequence. Every level in it appears, with its own heading, its own arm length and one extension per pair — and every one of those is a slider you drag. Nothing is searched and nothing is fetched: no engine picks a ring for you, no shelf is read, and the arms come out flush because you set their length rather than because something went looking for it.

The ks 2×1 admits k −2…3

Both spellings read the same. -1 1 -1 1 and -11-11 are the same four levels, so a link from a k board and a sequence you typed cannot mean two different stitches. A k outside −2…3 is kept in the field and marked — it is never quietly rounded into one the size admits.

What is real on this page. The base 2×1 ring is the committed geometry of artifacts/four-twists-2x1/ring.json, and every arm above it is placed by the fitter’s own arithmetic — the start slid along the parent’s line by the pair’s extension, both arms of a pair alike, the band’s heading adopted whole, the mirror arm taken by rotating 180° about the ring’s centre. The lengths, the spread and the gaps are measured off what you can see.

What is a stand-in. How a k becomes a heading. Here each unit of k turns its band 75°, and its pairs then open at the gap the bounds want — one division each. It is close in spirit to what the measured k = −1 stack does per level, so -1 1 -1 1 unwinds and rewinds. On the built page that number is the engine’s plan read once as a starting point — and then it is yours to drag, which is the whole argument for the page.
