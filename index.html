A 3D reimagining of OpenStrand Studio

Strands with a real thickness, stacked in Z.

OpenStrand draws laces from the top down and fakes over-and-under with masking. Scoubidou3D gives every strand a thickness, turns its layer index into a height, and turns each mask into a crossing where one lace physically lifts and the other dips — so you can tilt the camera and watch the weave interlock.

The question the project asks

What if a strand had a real thickness, and “layer over layer” in the layer panel meant the strand physically sits on top in space — so you could tilt the camera and see the weave in 3D?

That's the whole idea. Each strand becomes a ribbon, like the plastic-lacing gimp lanyards this was named after: its OpenStrand width runs across the ribbon, a new thickness runs through it, and its layer index becomes its height. Everything else follows from that one move.

The translation, in one table
OpenStrand (2D) Scoubidou3D
strand width ribbon width (across)
— ribbon thickness (through) new
layer order in the panel default height in Z — the top layer rides over
MaskedStrand (first over second) a real over/under weave: the over lace lifts, the under lace dips
attached strand (glued endpoint) a lofted connector bridging the layer gap
top-down canvas orbit camera, one click back to top view

What works today

Everything below is in the app right now — open it and try it.

  • 🧵

    Strands become 3D ribbons

    Every strand is extruded into a solid ribbon with configurable thickness, rounded edges, rounded ends and a stroke-coloured outline.

  • 🧶

    Masks become a real over/under weave

    The headline feature. Where OpenStrand paints one strand on top of another, Scoubidou3D makes it physical: at every crossing the over lace lifts and the under lace dips, so a single strand can go over one neighbour and under the next — a true basket weave, not a flat stack. Masks appear in the layer stack as their own rows, with flip and delete controls, and imported MaskedStrand records weave automatically.

  • 🪢

    Attachments are really connected

    A lofted bridge morphs the parent's end cross-section into the child's start across the layer gap, so the join reads as one continuous lace stepping up a storey.

  • 🪜

    Levels — storeys in the stack

    Drop a level row into the layer panel and everything above it rests a full storey higher — two strand thicknesses, the height of a woven round, so a lace sits on the round below instead of through it.

  • 🔗

    Attach, Move, Weave — editing in 3D

    Endpoints become grab handles. Pull a free endpoint to grow a new strand, drag a joined one and everything glued to it follows, or click two strands to say which goes over. Control points use OpenStrand's own marks and staging.

  • 📥

    Imports real OpenStrand files

    Load an OpenStrand Studio or OpenStrandJS .json save and see it in 3D. The curve math is a faithful port of the original, so shapes match.

  • 💾

    Save your own samples

    Arrange strands, pick the over/unders, then save — kept in your browser, still there after a refresh. Copy JSON hands you the scene as text. Nothing is uploaded.

  • 🎥

    Full 3D camera and live controls

    Orbit, pan and zoom, with one click back to the familiar top-down view — plus sliders for thickness, width, weave depth and span, layer lift, outline and grid.

How the weave works

Wherever two centerlines cross, we know who is over: a mask if one covers the pair, otherwise the higher layer. Each strand turns its crossings into a smooth Z height field, and the ribbon is swept along that undulating centerline. Two properties make it behave the way OpenStrand does.

A crossing sets an absolute height, not a nudge

The over lace goes to +h and the under lace to −h about the weave plane. A mask therefore means one purely local thing — this strand crosses over that one, here — and costs the same whether the two strands are neighbours in the layer panel or ten layers apart. Masking the bottom strand over the top one leaves every other layer untouched.

Overlapping crossings blend, they don't add

Where two crossings fall close together the heights are pulse-weighted-averaged, so neighbours pulling opposite ways resolve instead of cancelling or doubling. The three-way case falls out for free: the top lace lands at +h, the bottom at −h, and one caught between them settles in the middle.

Because each crossing is resolved on its own, cyclic weaves work — x over y, y over z and z over x is impossible for a rigid stack, but it is exactly what a real woven knot does.

The box stitch

The built-in Box stitch — starting stitch sample is the classic two-colour lanyard, caught at the moment the first stitch closes. Two laces are crossed and pinned, their four arms lettered A/B/C/D, and each arm folds back across the middle in turn — the last tucking under the first.

The two laces cross nine times and it needs exactly one mask. The arms were folded in layer order, so the stacking already tells the truth at eight of the nine crossings; the ninth is the move that locks the stitch, where the last arm dives back under the first one folded. That single contradiction gets a mask — precisely how you'd do it in OpenStrand Studio.

The geometry comes from a scene built by hand in the app, so the proportions are a real stitch rather than an idealised diagram.

Run it

The quickest path is the hosted build — open the app and start orbiting. To hack on it you need Node.js 18 or newer.

Develop

git clone https://github.com/ysetbon/Scoubidou3D.git
cd Scoubidou3D
npm install
npm run dev      # http://localhost:5173

Build the static site

npm run build    # outputs to dist/
npm run preview

The build produces this page at the root and the app at /app/. Pushing to main publishes both to GitHub Pages.
