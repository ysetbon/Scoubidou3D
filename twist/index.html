The twist study

The twist stitch, all 64 faces.

An m × n twist column is a woven block whose every loose end is carried out and re-paired. There are 64 faces from 1 × 1 to 8 × 8, and none of them was guessed: each one's turn comes out of a lace's own clearance, and what that costs is measured face by face rather than asserted.

What the retune bought, and what it cost

Each face has two candidate angles, one per family of laces, and they agree only when m = n. The family you see is now laid at its own angle instead of the one that suited the few. That is a choice about which flaw is worse to look at, not a derivation.

The last row is the honest one. Gaps do not only open — on a 4 × 8 one closes to 1.1 px, which is two 46 px laces almost exactly on top of each other. The overhang is roughly halved everywhere and that is the visible win; what it is traded against is real, and it is recorded per face in the repository so it cannot drift unnoticed.
